# Juris: read me

**Status: live on beta.**

**For:** lawyers. Juris helps a lawyer map how a case progresses and document every event that happened in it.

**Map:** a mind map. The case is the central node. Every other node attaches to the centre or to another node, and every chain leads back to the centre. Nothing floats free.

## How it works

1. **A point comes in.** It gets a kind (filing, hearing, order, evidence, or what happened) and a date, and is attached to the case or to an earlier point.
2. **Linked.** Every point must lead back to the case. A loose point raises a note.
3. **Relevance.** A point that reads far from the rest of the case raises a soft note.
4. **Dates and facts.** An outcome dated before what it came from, and two points about the same thing with different dates or figures, raise notes.
5. **Evidence.** Evidence nothing relates to yet raises a soft note.
6. **The map.** Filings, hearings, orders and evidence go clockwise from 12 o'clock by date; what happened sits on the outer rings within its parent's slice.

Safeguards: the AI never invents dates, citations or law and never predicts an outcome. Kāhyo helps organise a case; it is not legal advice.
